About

I’m a backend engineer with 8 years of experience working on production backend systems, APIs, deployment workflows, async workloads, and operational debugging within the .NET ecosystem.

Most of my work has involved systems that were already operating at scale — systems with existing operational history, reliability tradeoffs, deployment pressure, debugging friction, and growing complexity over time.

Over time I found myself drawn to the kinds of backend problems that are difficult to explain. APIs become slower without an obvious bottleneck. Deployments start creating risk that nobody can clearly account for. Production issues consume engineering time even though monitoring and logging already exist.

These are usually not implementation problems. They are understanding problems. Before a team can improve a system, it needs a clear picture of what is actually happening and why.

What I Work With

My experience has primarily involved production backend systems with:

  • API services and backend workflows
  • Async job processing and background workloads
  • Deployment and release coordination workflows
  • Caching behavior and performance bottlenecks
  • Operational debugging and investigation workflows
  • Monitoring, logging, and observability gaps
  • Reliability and maintainability concerns in existing systems

I’m particularly interested in backend problems where multiple systems, workflows, or operational decisions interact in ways that make the real cause difficult to identify.

Why Diagnosis Comes First

Teams under delivery pressure often move directly toward fixes before fully understanding the operational dynamics behind a problem. That can temporarily reduce visible symptoms while leaving the underlying issue unresolved.

I intentionally separate engagements into two phases:

  • Investigation & Diagnosis — identifying bottlenecks, operational pressure points, investigation gaps, and realistic improvement priorities
  • Improvement Assistance — helping teams evaluate and implement practical operational improvements without introducing unnecessary complexity

The goal is not to produce large consulting reports. The goal is to help teams understand what is actually happening in their systems and move toward clearer, more maintainable operational behavior.

Typical Situations

  • API latency has gradually increased but the team cannot identify a single bottleneck.
  • Production incidents take significantly longer to diagnose than they should.
  • Deployments regularly introduce uncertainty, rollback pressure, or unexpected behavior.
  • Teams spend increasing amounts of time firefighting recurring operational issues.
  • Monitoring exists, but it still isn't clear why problems occur.

How I Work

Engagements are async-first, scoped, and intentionally focused.

I typically work from the operational context a team can provide — metrics, logs, traces, deployment history, architecture context, and observations from previous debugging attempts.

I’m not positioned as a replacement for an engineering team or as an embedded contractor. The value I provide is an outside operational perspective focused on investigation clarity, backend reliability, and practical improvement guidance.

Since this practice runs alongside my full-time backend engineering work, I take on a limited number of engagements selectively.
