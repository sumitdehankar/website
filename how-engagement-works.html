How Engagement Works

Engagements are intentionally lightweight, scoped, and focused on helping teams understand backend problems before deciding what improvements are worth making.

Most engagements follow a simple two-phase approach: diagnosis first, followed by optional improvement assistance.

1. Initial Conversation

We discuss the problem your team is experiencing, what has already been tried, available technical context, and whether the situation is a good fit for this type of engagement.

2. Scope Definition

We agree on the specific problem being examined, available data sources, expected deliverables, timelines, and communication approach before any work begins.

3. Diagnosis

I review available metrics, logs, traces, deployment history, architecture context, operational workflows, and previous observations to understand what is happening and identify likely contributing factors.

4. Findings & Recommendations

You receive a summary of observations, likely causes, unresolved questions, and prioritized recommendations for improvement.

The goal is to provide enough clarity for your team to make informed engineering decisions rather than adding another layer of uncertainty.

5. Optional Improvement Assistance

Some teams choose to stop after diagnosis. Others want help evaluating potential improvements.

Improvement assistance may include architecture discussions, review of proposed changes, implementation tradeoff guidance, deployment workflow recommendations, and follow-up clarification sessions.

It does not include embedded engineering work, ongoing production support, full implementation ownership, or long-term operational responsibility.

Engagement Structure

Engagements are typically split into two phases:

  • Diagnosis & Findings — understanding the problem, reviewing available evidence, identifying likely causes, and producing prioritized recommendations.
  • Improvement Assistance (Optional) — helping your team evaluate proposed changes, implementation approaches, and operational improvements based on the findings.

Some teams only need diagnosis. Others continue with focused improvement support. Both approaches are possible.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you implement fixes?

No. My role is helping teams understand problems clearly and evaluate practical improvements. Your engineering team remains responsible for implementation decisions and delivery.

Do you need access to production systems?

Not necessarily. Many engagements can begin using logs, metrics, architecture context, deployment history, and operational observations provided by the team.

Can engagements be fully remote?

Yes. Engagements are designed to be async-first and remote-friendly.

Do you work with startups or larger teams?

Both. The common factor is a backend operational problem that requires structured diagnosis and practical improvement planning.

Not Sure If It's A Good Fit?

If you're dealing with a backend problem that has resisted obvious fixes, start with a conversation. In many cases, determining whether an engagement makes sense only takes a brief discussion.
